CEO Highlights & Lowlights July 2026
▼  Lowlights
SMB came in well below the month's targets, reaching 54.9% of the New MRR goal ($38.8K) and 60.9% of New Logos (1,519). The shortfall concentrated in the Dominican Republic (25% of revenue target) and Spain (45%), both still ramping ahead of upcoming e-invoicing and Verifactu mandates.
Several POS outages during the month weighed on the customer experience, with POS Support CSAT falling 25 points. Restoring and safeguarding POS stability is a priority as we scale the product; the team is working to prevent recurrence.
The Dominican Republic remains our highest-CAC market ($510 blended, with paid media above $1,100). In response, we're using the newly enabled ICP segmentation to direct investment toward the segments that are converting.
Mexico grew below budget despite improved acquisition efficiency. Contactability remains the binding constraint — outbound numbers flagged as spam and low answer rates are compressing PQL-to-logo conversion. Channel experiments and a local trust-building strategy are now underway.
A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck Colombia — our largest market — on August 10, prompting a national state of disaster. Our immediate priority is the safety and support of team members and their families. As an external force-majeure event, some short-term commercial impact is expected; the full effect is still being assessed as response efforts continue.
Financial Update
July closed strong: revenue reached $2.79M, +13.7% vs budget and +40.4% YoY, though roughly half of that beat is FX (COP appreciation) rather than organic growth — real local-currency growth was closer to 5–6%. Gross margin expanded to 83.5% (+1.7pp vs budget), helped by a one-off AWS accrual reversal and a favorable payment-processor mix. EBITDA hit $486K — 3.1x budget and the best month of the year — as the revenue beat flowed straight through with OpEx essentially on plan (-0.3%). Cash closed at $4.68M, +162% vs budget and the highest balance of 2026, the 7th straight month above plan. YTD EBITDA margin swung from -9.9% to +11.2% (+21pp YoY), putting the full year on track to beat budget meaningfully ($3.2M EBITDA projected vs $1.3M budgeted). Bottom line: a genuinely healthy month operationally, with FX acting as a tailwind worth flagging rather than banking on going forward.
✦  Highlights
Overall churn held at 3.9% in July (Core 2.4%, Lite 4.9%) — the results demonstrate the effectiveness of our retention strategy despite challenges in specific markets.
Sales leadership is now in place after a period of interim coverage. The incoming Head of Sales restores dedicated ownership of commercial execution and attainment, completing the Revenue leadership structure.
AI-driven optimization reduced Support headcount by 17% versus January while sustaining service volume — the team now handles more customer demand per person, a step-change in cost-to-serve and scalability.
AI-powered Quality Assurance at scale. The launch of the in-house AI QA solution increased audit coverage from 30% of calls reviewed manually to 100% of sales calls, providing complete visibility into commercial quality and enabling more consistent, data-driven coaching.
Faster sales onboarding. Apex Academy cut the average time for new sales reps to close their first deal from 30 to 7 days, accelerating ramp-up.
In Costa Rica, ICP targeting is translating into quality, not just volume. Logo growth reached 32% (small) and 64% (medium) among ideal-profile clients, and the qualified-client mix rose from 54% to 68% — an early signal that selective acquisition is working.
The demand engine had its strongest month of the year in July. Sign-ups grew +25.4% (36,061) and MQLs +14.0% (18,556) — the best traffic month of 2026 — with direct traffic stabilizing after two soft months and lead nurturing rebounding to its best conversion in weeks.

Transfer Pricing: The 2026 DEMPE Model
Transfer Pricing — 2 of 3
02 / 05
Why we restructured. The prior cost-sharing model (2022–2025) lacked strategic direction, leaked tax through inefficient withholding/double-taxation management, didn't capture the value of the Alegra software, and had weak arm's-length documentation. New direction: rebuild around the Alegra intangible itself — a DEMPE-based structure, defensible under OECD transfer pricing guidelines.
The 2026 Model — Four Instruments
01

Co-Ownership of the Intangible

DEMPE-based split: 99% Soluciones Alegra (Colombia) · 1% Alegra Contabilidad (Mexico), driven by MRR by jurisdiction.

▶
02

Sub-Licensing

Non-exclusive licenses to 8 operating jurisdictions: Argentina, Chile, Costa Rica, Panama, Peru, Dominican Rep., Spain, U.S.

▶
03

Royalties

10% of monthly billed revenue, paid by each sub-licensee to its co-owner for use of the intangible.

▶
04

Technical Services

Intercompany billing for development/support work, 8% markup under Cost Plus / TNMM.

The Alegra intangible is the single center of gravity for the entire model.
