CEO Highlights & Lowlights August 2026
▼  Lowlights
SMB came in well below the month's targets, reaching 54.9% of the New MRR goal ($38.8K) and 60.9% of New Logos (1,519). The largest gap sat in the Dominican Republic, which closed at 25% of its revenue target while still ramping ahead of the upcoming e-invoicing mandate.
DR's high CAC ($510 blended, >$1,100 paid) reflects the DGII e-CF mandate not yet being enforced, not channel inefficiency. The signal: keep budget mitigated until obligation kicks in, then ramp aggressively, leveraging the newly enabled ICP segmentation to direct spend toward converting segments. Goal: bring paid CAC back to $400 USD and blended CAC to $300 USD, improving both further from there.
Several POS outages during the month weighed on the customer experience, with POS Support CSAT falling 25 points. Restoring and safeguarding POS stability is a priority as we scale the product; the team is working to prevent recurrence.
Mexico grew below budget despite improved acquisition efficiency. Contactability remains the binding constraint — outbound numbers flagged as spam and low answer rates are compressing MQL-to-logo conversion. Dialing experiments and a local trust-building strategy are now underway.
A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck Colombia — our largest market — on August 10, prompting a national state of disaster. Our immediate priority is the safety and support of team members and their families. As an external force-majeure event, some short-term commercial impact is expected; the full effect is still being assessed as response efforts continue.
Financial Update
July closed strong: revenue reached $2.79M, +13.7% vs budget and +40.4% YoY, though roughly half of that beat is FX (COP appreciation) rather than organic growth — real local-currency growth was closer to 5–6%. Gross margin expanded to 83.5% (+1.7pp vs budget), helped by a one-off AWS accrual reversal and a favorable payment-processor mix. EBITDA hit $486K — 3.1x budget and the best month of the year — as the revenue beat flowed straight through with OpEx essentially on plan (-0.3%). Cash closed at $4.68M, +162% vs budget and the highest balance of 2026, the 7th straight month above plan. YTD EBITDA margin swung from -9.9% to +11.2% (+21pp YoY), putting the full year on track to beat budget meaningfully ($3.2M EBITDA projected vs $1.3M budgeted). Bottom line: a genuinely healthy month operationally, with FX acting as a tailwind worth flagging rather than banking on going forward.
✦  Highlights
Overall churn held at 3.9% in July (Core 2.4%, Lite 4.9%) — the results demonstrate the effectiveness of our adoption strategy despite challenges in specific markets.
Sales leadership is now in place after a period of interim coverage. The incoming Head of Sales restores dedicated ownership of commercial execution and attainment, completing the Revenue leadership structure.
AI-driven optimization reduced Support headcount by 17% versus January while sustaining service volume — the team now handles more customer demand per person, a step-change in cost-to-serve and scalability.
Commercial:
AI-powered quality assurance now covers 100% of sales calls, up from 30% reviewed manually.
Apex Academy cut time-to-first-deal for new reps from 30 days to 7.
ICP-focused targeting in Costa Rica lifted the qualified-client mix from 54% to 68%.
The demand engine had its strongest month of the year in July. Sign-ups grew +25.4% (36,061) and MQLs +14.0% (18,556) — the best traffic month of 2026 — with direct traffic stabilizing after two soft months and lead nurturing rebounding to its best conversion in weeks.

Our upsell isn't strategic yet — it rides the customer's income

Discussion topic · 2/3 · Upsell
What moves net upsell · plan changes vs. customer income (FX-neutral)
~6 in 10 of upsell reverses

The scatter shows net upsell rising in high-income months and falling in low-income ones — much of it is customers crossing a billing threshold in a strong month, not us delivering more value. It's seasonal (peaks in December, drops in January).

So the same accounts bounce up and back down: most upsell comes from accounts that also downgrade later (chronic oscillators). It's oscillation on the customer's income cycle — not durable expansion.

Today's upsell isn't something we drive — it's the customer's income cycle. They upgrade when they cross a billing limit in a good month, then revert when income drops. The strategic shift: move from upsell by billing to upsell by value — tie upgrades to features and usage, not the customer's revenue.

We need to build the expansion machine

Discussion topic · 3/3 · The strategy
The strategy · pricing + product + process, working together

No single lever fixes this. Expansion has to become a system — pricing, product and the commercial team giving and using clear value levers, not one-off reactions.

1Be intentional with pricing
Our strongest lever. Easing this year was a deliberate call — and the data says the churn we feared from price increases does not necessarily stick. So there's room to push. The question is when and how much, not whether.
biggest lever · room to push
2Give the team value to sell
Upsell tied to usage the customer keeps — users, warehouses, API, storage, AI — and a second product that sticks: today customers buy Payroll/POS then drop it and keep Accounting, costing ~1pt of expansion. Without value levers from pricing & product, a CS quota alone is empty.
2nd product = ~1pt at stake
3Build a real expansion process
Today it's reactive — we sell when the customer asks. Build systematic account identification, triggers, qualification, offers and follow-up — the KAM / Account Management + CS motion. That turns upsell/cross-sell into something we generate, not something we wait for.
proactive · KAM + CS
None of these levers work alone — expansion becomes structural only when pricing, product and process move together. That's the shift: from ad hoc pushes to a system the commercial team can run every quarter.

Action plan — country-specific

Discussion topic · 11/11
The decision was the same in both countries — cut Paid Media to look for efficiencies. Colombia benefited because it already had the brand for Direct Traffic to fill the gap. Mexico needs to build that same brand cushion first.

Colombia — protect and understand the brand asset

1

Confirm the December 2025 Paid cutback was the trigger — and that the Direct Traffic gain really comes from brand awareness, not another coinciding cause.

2

Document the level of brand/PR/positioning investment sustaining that Direct Traffic — it's the real asset behind the result.

3

Watch that the current Paid level (35-45%) is sustainable — if Direct Traffic depends on brand investment that gets cut, the effect could reverse.

Mexico — build brand before replicating the cutback

1

Diagnose Mexico's current brand awareness/positioning versus Colombia's — the variable that explains why the same cutback didn't produce the same mix effect.

2

Evaluate an investment in brand building (PR, presence, awareness) before expecting Direct Traffic to absorb a larger Paid reduction.

3

In the meantime, keep documenting the efficiency gain within Paid itself — it's a real win, even if it's not the same lever that worked in Colombia.
